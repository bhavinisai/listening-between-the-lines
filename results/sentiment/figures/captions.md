# Sentiment figures — captions

All figures are generated by `src/sentiment/figures/make_figures.py` from saved results only (no training, no new experiments, no tuning). PNG, 300 dpi, 6.5 in wide. Plotted numbers are in `data/`. Retained models: valence trained on 1,783 examples and arousal trained on 5,000 examples (development-selected on the validation set).

### Performance by Training Size

**Figure 1. Performance by training size.** Validation macro-F1 of valence (left) and arousal (right) models trained on nested sets of 1,783, 3,000 and 5,000 LLM-labeled turns, evaluated on the fixed validation set (256 turns from 18 validation episodes, disjoint from the training episodes; labels are the three-pass LLM majority). Open circles show the three individual training seeds; filled circles with bars show the mean ± one standard deviation across seeds. Mean macro-F1 was 0.787, 0.773 and 0.754 for valence and 0.519, 0.566 and 0.618 for arousal. With only three seeds the standard deviation is itself imprecise; the vertical axes are truncated to make seed-to-seed spread visible. Each model is the best-validation epoch of its run, so the scores are optimistic development estimates, not held-out test performance.

*File:* `performance_by_training_size.png` · *Suggested placement:* Results — data scaling · *Source data:* `data/performance_by_training_size.csv`

### Valence Data Comparison

**Figure 4. Valence data comparison.** Validation macro-F1 (left) and negative-class recall (right) for the baseline valence model (1,782 labeled turns) and for models trained with 1,106 additional LLM-labeled turns drawn either from targeted rare-class strata (+ Targeted) or from the random stratum (+ Random). ### Valence Ablation Results

**Figure 5. Valence ablation results.** Differences in validation macro-F1, negative-class recall and negative-class precision between the three conditions of Figure 4. Points are mean bootstrap differences and bars are 95% percentile confidence intervals; the dashed line marks no difference. The resampling unit is the validation episode (18 episodes, 2,000 resamples), and within each resample every condition's metric is averaged over its five seeds, so the intervals reflect uncertainty over episodes and not seed variability. Macro-F1: targeted − baseline −0.022 [−0.049, −0.0001]; random − baseline −0.031 [−0.043, −0.019]; targeted − random +0.009 [−0.018, +0.027]. Negative recall: targeted − baseline +0.040 [−0.016, +0.094]; targeted − random +0.063 [+0.008, +0.119]. Evaluation: the fixed validation set (256 turns from 18 validation episodes, disjoint from the training episodes; labels are the three-pass LLM majority). Only 18 episodes and 46 negative turns underlie the intervals, several of which lie close to zero; no multiplicity correction was applied and the intervals are descriptive.

*File:* `valence_ablation_results.png` · *Suggested placement:* Results — valence ablation · *Source data:* `data/valence_ablation_results.csv`

### Single Model vs. Ensemble

**Figure 8. Single model versus ensemble.** Top: validation macro-F1 of the retained single model, the average over the individual seed models, and the equal-weight probability ensemble of those seed models (valence: five seeds of the 1,783-example set, 0.795, 0.790, 0.780; arousal: three seeds of the 5,000-example set, 0.626, 0.618, 0.621). Bottom: ensemble minus comparator macro-F1 with 95% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals (resampling unit: the 18 validation episodes, 2,000 resamples); the dashed line marks no difference. Valence: ensemble − single model −0.014 [−0.036, +0.007], ensemble − seed average −0.010 [−0.021, +0.004]; arousal: −0.004 [−0.038, +0.036] and −0.001 [−0.027, +0.023]. Evaluation: the fixed validation set (256 turns from 18 validation episodes, disjoint from the training episodes; labels are the three-pass LLM majority). The ensemble was not selected; the retained models remain the single models. The single model was the best seed on these validation data, so it is a favourable comparator, and the ensemble members share training data, so their errors are correlated.

### Performance by Gender Dyad

**Figure 9. Performance by gender dyad.** Validation macro-F1 of the retained valence (left) and arousal (right) models for each gender dyad, as coded in the dataset (e.g., Male → Female); subgroup sizes are shown as n. Evaluation: the fixed validation set (256 turns from 18 validation episodes, disjoint from the training episodes; labels are the three-pass LLM majority); valence subgroup sizes: Male → Male n = 99, Male → Female n = 44, Female → Male n = 48, Female → Female n = 65. Macro-F1 is computed over all three classes in every subgroup, so values are comparable in definition across subgroups. It is undefined when a class has no true examples in the subgroup and is then not plotted (arousal, Male → Female: no high-arousal turns). Open markers flag subgroups in which at least one class has fewer than five true turns, where the score is unreliable (arousal, Female → Male: only 1 high-arousal turn). Per-subgroup class counts, flags, and the earlier present-class-only values are in the companion table. No confidence intervals were estimated; differences between dyads are descriptive and cannot be read as evidence of bias or its absence.

*File:* `performance_by_gender_dyad.png` · *Suggested placement:* Appendix — subgroup performance · *Source data:* `data/performance_by_gender_dyad.csv`

### Global limitations

1. Unless a caption states otherwise, performance is development-validation performance on 256 turns from 18 episodes; each run's checkpoint is its best-validation epoch, so scores are optimistic and are not held-out test estimates.
2. Labels are LLM-annotator majorities (three passes of one model family); agreement with the single-annotator human-gold labels was weak in earlier analyses.
3. Few seeds (3–5) were used; bootstrap intervals resample only the 18 validation episodes. No hypothesis tests were run and no multiplicity correction was applied; intervals are descriptive.
